# What is FDE? (plain English)

## Simple meaning

**FDE = Forward Deployed Engineer**

Think of it like this:

- A normal software engineer builds the product in the company office / cloud.
- An FDE goes **forward** (closer to the customer), **deploys** and adapts the product there, and makes it work for that customer’s real world.

“Forward deployed” comes from military language: people placed near the front line. In tech, the “front line” is the **customer site / customer problem**.

## What problem does FDE solve?

Companies buy powerful software (AI platforms, data tools, security tools, etc.), but often:

- it does not fit their data
- it does not fit their security rules
- their teams do not know how to use it
- their workflow is different from the demo

If nobody bridges that gap, the customer pays money and gets little value.

**The FDE is that bridge.**

## What an FDE actually does

In plain English, an FDE:

1. Talks to the customer and learns the real pain
2. Looks at their systems, data, and constraints
3. Designs a solution that can ship soon
4. Writes code / integrations / scripts / agents
5. Deploys it in the customer environment (or a safe copy of it)
6. Tests with real users
7. Fixes issues fast
8. Hands a stable pattern back to the core product team when possible

## FDE is not only “support”

Support answers tickets.  
Sales demo shows slides.  
FDE **builds**.

If you cannot code, debug, design, and ship, you are not doing FDE work.

## FDE is not only “consulting”

Consultants often write recommendations.  
FDEs leave behind **working software**.

## Why companies hire FDEs

Because modern products (especially AI) need customization:

- customer data is messy
- policies and compliance matter
- workflows differ by company
- demos are easy; production is hard

FDEs make the product succeed after the sale.

## Famous example (for intuition)

Palantir made the “Forward Deployed Engineer” title well known.  
Their idea: put elite builders next to the mission, not only behind a product roadmap.

Many AI companies now use similar titles:

- Forward Deployed Engineer
- Forward Deployed Software Engineer
- Deployment Engineer
- Customer Engineer (more demos/solutions; sometimes overlapping)
- Solutions Architect (more design; less day-to-day coding)

Names differ. The core idea is the same: **engineer close to the customer, shipping real outcomes**.

## Success looks like

- Customer can use the system in daily work
- Clear technical ownership and runbooks
- Measurable outcome (time saved, errors reduced, cases closed faster)
- Core product team learns what to productize next

## Failure looks like

- Cool demo, no adoption
- One-off scripts nobody can maintain
- FDE becomes permanent unpaid support
- No feedback loop to product

## Remember this line for interviews

> “An FDE is an engineer who ships customer outcomes, not just features.”
